The lines follow a field that flows around each stone. Nothing is drawn by hand: move a stone and the garden is raked again, stroke by stroke. Tap the gravel to set a stone, drag to move it, double-tap to take it away. Lines flow around each stone, raked stroke by stroke. Tap to set a stone, drag to move, double-tap to remove.

How it works

The field

Each stone sits in a moss island. Close to the stones the grooves are rings: their phase is the distance to the islands, joined with a smooth minimum, so two stones close together share one set of ripples. Further out the phase is a stream function, straight lines bent by the flow round a cylinder at each stone. Lines that would enter the rings end in the outer groove.

The gravel remembers

The height of the gravel lives in a texture. A rake stroke writes the field for the current stones only where the rake has passed. Everything not yet raked keeps its old pattern, so a moved stone leaves rings round nothing until the monk reaches them.

Re-raking

A change marks the rings and the bands of straight lines it touches. The monk rakes the rings first, then the bands from the far side to the near side, back and forth. A stroke takes about two seconds; a full re-rake after one change is roughly 20 to 25 strokes, about a minute. With reduced motion, re-raking is instant.
